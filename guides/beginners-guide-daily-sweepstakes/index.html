A Beginner's Guide to Entering Sweepstakes Daily

By the DailyClaimIt team · Published October 15, 2026

Entering sweepstakes is a popular hobby, sometimes called "sweeping." Some people enter a few promotions a week for fun; others build a daily routine. Winning is never guaranteed, and the odds for any single sweepstakes can be long. But with an organized approach, you can enter efficiently, avoid scams, and enjoy the occasional win. Here's how to get started.

Set expectations first

Every legitimate sweepstakes is a game of chance. Your odds depend on how many eligible entries are received, which you usually can't know in advance. Entering more sweepstakes over time gives you more chances, but there's no system that guarantees prizes. Treat it as a free hobby with fun upside, not a way to make money.

Step 1: Set up your sweepstakes toolkit

  • A dedicated email address just for entries. Check it daily so you don't miss winner notifications. See protecting your privacy.
  • A browser autofill profile with your entry details (name, address, phone) so forms take seconds. Double-check autofill for accuracy; a wrong address or phone could disqualify you.
  • A simple tracker: a spreadsheet or notes app with columns for sweepstakes name, sponsor, end date, entry frequency, and a link to the official rules.
  • A password manager if you'll create accounts on sweepstakes or brand sites.

Step 2: Understand entry frequencies

Official rules tell you how often you can enter. Common patterns:

  • Single entry: enter once for the whole promotion.
  • Daily entry: enter once per day, often defined by a specific time zone. Check whether "day" resets at midnight Eastern or another time.
  • Weekly or other periods.
  • Bonus entries: extra entries for actions like referring friends or following accounts, if the rules allow.

Never exceed the limits. Using multiple email addresses or automated tools to enter more than allowed usually violates the rules and can get all your entries disqualified. Most rules also prohibit entering through automated scripts or bots.

Step 3: Find legitimate sweepstakes

  • Brand websites and newsletters often announce promotions.
  • Product packaging and receipts sometimes include codes or entry info, and there should be a free alternative method of entry.
  • Official social media accounts of brands you follow.
  • Sweepstakes directories and listing sites, including our Sweepstakes page.

Whatever the source, check that each promotion has official rules and an identifiable sponsor. Our guide to how online sweepstakes work explains what to look for in the rules.

Step 4: Build a 15-minute daily routine

  1. Check your sweepstakes inbox for winner notifications first (2 minutes). Verify anything that looks like a win before clicking links.
  2. Enter your daily sweepstakes from your tracker, in order (8–10 minutes). Bookmarking them in a single folder lets you open them quickly.
  3. Add new ones you've found, after reading the rules (3 minutes).
  4. Remove expired ones from your tracker.

A fixed routine prevents the hobby from eating more time than you want to spend on it.

Step 5: Prioritize smartly

  • Local or regional sweepstakes may have fewer entrants than national ones.
  • Promotions with many smaller prizes give more chances to win something.
  • Effort-based entries (like writing a short answer or uploading a photo) may attract fewer entrants. If winners are judged on merit, that's a contest rather than a sweepstakes, and quality matters.
  • Prizes you actually want. Remember that prizes are generally taxable; see sweepstakes taxes.

Step 6: Stay safe

  • Never pay to enter or to claim a prize. The FTC says real prizes are free.
  • Never give bank or card details to claim a prize.
  • Be suspicious of wins from promotions you didn't enter, which is another reason a tracker helps.
  • Read our full guide: how to spot fake sweepstakes and giveaway scams.

Step 7: Respond quickly if you win

Winner notifications often have response deadlines. If you miss the deadline, the prize may go to an alternate. When you get a legitimate notification:

  • compare it to the official rules (sponsor, prize, notification method);
  • complete any required affidavit or release through the official process;
  • keep copies of everything for tax time.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Entering more often than the rules allow.
  • Using an email you rarely check, then missing a win.
  • Entering promotions you're not eligible for (wrong state or age).
  • Typos in autofilled contact details.
  • Getting burned out by trying to enter everything. Consistency beats volume.

Keep it fun

The people who enjoy sweepstakes most tend to treat it lightly: a quick daily habit, a few favorite promotions, and genuine excitement when a small prize shows up. If it starts to feel like a chore, scale back.

Frequently asked questions

How many sweepstakes should a beginner enter?

Start small. A list of 10 to 20 daily-entry promotions plus a few single-entry ones is plenty to build a habit. Add more only if your routine still feels quick and enjoyable.

Do I have to give my phone number?

It depends on the promotion. Some sponsors require a phone number to contact winners. Read the consent language to see whether you're also agreeing to marketing calls or texts, which is often optional.

Is it legal to enter sweepstakes from any state?

Eligibility is set in the official rules. Many national sweepstakes are open to residents of the 50 US states and DC, but some exclude certain states or are limited to specific regions. Always check eligibility before entering.
