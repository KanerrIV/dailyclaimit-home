Protecting Your Privacy When Entering Giveaways and Freebies

By the DailyClaimIt team · Published October 15, 2026

Sweepstakes, giveaways, surveys, and free sample offers almost always ask for some personal information. That's normal: sponsors need to contact winners and ship prizes, and many promotions exist partly to build marketing lists. The trick is to share what's needed, protect what isn't, and keep your main inbox and accounts safe. Here's a practical approach.

1. Use a separate email address

This is the single most useful step. Create a free email account just for sweepstakes, freebies, and rewards sites. Benefits:

  • Your personal and work inboxes stay clean.
  • Winner notifications don't get buried among other mail, as long as you check this account regularly.
  • If the address ends up on lots of marketing lists, it doesn't affect your important accounts.
  • It's easier to spot phishing: a "prize" email sent to your personal address that you never used for entries is a big red flag.

Some email providers also support "plus addressing" (for example yourname+giveaways@...), which can help you see which sites share your address. Not every site accepts it, so a dedicated account is the more reliable option.

2. Share only what's necessary

For most entries, a name, email, and sometimes a mailing address, age or birth date, and phone number are typical. Be cautious about anything more:

  • Never give your Social Security number just to enter. A legitimate sponsor may need tax information from a confirmed winner of a larger prize, through an official process; see sweepstakes taxes.
  • Never give bank account or card details to "claim" a prize. The FTC says if you have to pay or give financial information to get a prize, it's a scam.
  • Think twice about optional fields. If a field isn't required, you can usually skip it.

3. Consider a separate phone number

Some offers ask for a phone number and may send marketing texts or calls if you consent. Read the consent checkbox carefully; marketing consent is often optional. Some people use a free secondary number from an internet calling service for entries. You can also reply STOP to unwanted marketing texts from legitimate senders.

4. Read the privacy policy and consent language

You don't need to read every word, but look for:

  • whether your information will be shared with or sold to third parties or "marketing partners";
  • whether you're agreeing to calls or texts;
  • how to unsubscribe or request deletion.

Lead-generation sweepstakes often share entrant information with partners; that's how they fund prizes. That can be a fair trade, but it should be a choice you make knowingly.

5. Watch out for phishing

Once you start entering giveaways, you'll get more emails saying you won things, and some will be fake. Signs of phishing:

  • A prize from a promotion you don't remember entering.
  • Urgent deadlines and threats that you'll "lose" the prize.
  • Requests to log in through a link, pay a fee, or share financial details.
  • Sender addresses that don't match the sponsor's official domain.

When in doubt, don't click. Go to the sponsor's site directly and check the official rules and winner contact process. Our scam-spotting guide has more detail.

6. Secure the accounts you do use

  • Use a unique password for each rewards or sweepstakes account. A password manager makes this easy.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it's offered, especially on email and on accounts that hold cash balances or gift cards.
  • Keep the email account you use for entries secure too; if it's compromised, someone could claim your prizes or reset other accounts.

7. Be careful with social media giveaways

Giveaways that ask you to like, follow, tag friends, or share often make your activity public. Impersonator accounts may then message you claiming you won. Only trust announcements from the official, verified account, and never pay "shipping" to a stranger. Consider whether you're comfortable with the visibility of your entries.

8. Clean up periodically

  • Unsubscribe from lists you no longer want using the unsubscribe link in legitimate emails.
  • Delete accounts on rewards sites you've stopped using.
  • Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask companies to delete your data. Check their privacy policy for instructions.

9. Protect kids and family members

Most sweepstakes and rewards offers require entrants to be adults, often 18 or older. Don't enter on behalf of children, and talk with older family members about prize scams, which often target people who aren't used to online promotions.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to give my home address for a sweepstakes?

For legitimate promotions with official rules and an identifiable sponsor, providing a mailing address is common and usually needed to send prizes. Be more careful with unknown sites that have no rules or privacy policy.

What should I do if I start getting spam calls after entering giveaways?

Review which entries asked for your phone number and what you consented to. Reply STOP to marketing texts from legitimate senders, and consider registering your number with the National Do Not Call Registry at DoNotCall.gov, which limits calls from many telemarketers.
