How to Make Extra Money With Rewards Apps

By the DailyClaimIt team · Published October 15, 2026

Rewards apps promise points for everyday activities: answering surveys, playing mobile games, shopping online, watching videos, or trying new apps. The good ones really do pay, usually in gift cards or cash via PayPal. But the amounts are typically modest, and some tasks have fine print that matters. This guide explains how rewards apps work and how to use them sensibly.

How rewards apps make money (and pay you)

Rewards apps are essentially marketing middlemen. Advertisers pay them when users take certain actions, such as installing a game and reaching a level, signing up for a service, or making a purchase through a link. Market research firms pay them for completed surveys. The app keeps part of that revenue and shares part with you as points.

That's why the highest-paying tasks are usually the ones most valuable to advertisers, like reaching a high level in a game or signing up for a paid service. It also explains why rewards can change frequently: they depend on what advertisers are paying at the moment.

Common ways to earn

  • Surveys: steady but small rewards; you'll be screened out of some. See legit paid survey sites.
  • Game offers: install a game and reach a specific milestone within a time limit. These can pay more but often take many hours, and some milestones are much easier with in-app purchases.
  • Shopping cashback: earn points for purchases made through the app's links. Useful only for purchases you'd make anyway.
  • Sign-up offers: join a service or start a trial. Some are free; others require payment or a card. Read the conditions carefully.
  • Daily activities: check-ins, polls, quizzes, or videos that earn small amounts. See are paid quizzes legit?
  • Referrals: bonuses when friends join, subject to the app's rules.

Realistic expectations

Most people earn small amounts from rewards apps. A realistic goal is something like a gift card every so often, not a steady income. Earnings vary widely depending on where you live, your demographics, which tasks are available, and how much time you put in. Be skeptical of screenshots or videos claiming large, consistent earnings, especially if the person is also promoting a referral link.

A simple way to stay grounded is to estimate your effective hourly rate: divide what you earned by the time you actually spent, including screen-outs and waiting. If the number feels too low for your time, that's a sign to be more selective, or to treat the app as something you only use during downtime.

Read the fine print on offers

Before starting any offer, especially game and sign-up offers, check:

  • Exact requirements: what milestone you need to reach, and by when.
  • New users only: many offers only credit you if you've never installed the app or used the service before.
  • Purchase requirements: whether the reward requires spending money, and whether it's worth it.
  • Tracking and pending periods: rewards can take time to credit, and some stay "pending" for days or weeks.
  • Tracking tips: apps often recommend disabling ad blockers and completing the offer on the same device and browser where you clicked it.

Taking a screenshot of the offer terms when you start can help if you need to contact support about a missing reward.

Picking a legit rewards app

  • Free to join, with no fees to cash out beyond any clearly disclosed minimums.
  • Transparent cash-out options and minimums listed before you start.
  • Responsive support and a clear process for missing rewards.
  • Consistent independent reviews across app stores and consumer forums. Look for patterns rather than single complaints.
  • A clear privacy policy. Rewards apps can request a lot of permissions. Only grant what makes sense.

Protect your time, money and data

  • Don't spend money to earn points unless it's something you'd buy anyway.
  • Cancel trials you don't want before they renew. Put a reminder in your calendar the day you sign up.
  • Use a separate email address for rewards activity. See protecting your privacy.
  • Review app permissions on your phone and turn off anything unnecessary.
  • Never share account logins with anyone offering to "boost" your earnings.
  • Stick to one account. Most apps prohibit multiple accounts per person or household and may close accounts that break this rule.

Taxes

Rewards and cash earned from apps are generally income in the US. Many people earn too little for the platform to issue a tax form, but that doesn't change whether the income is taxable. Our overview of sweepstakes and rewards taxes explains the reporting thresholds. Not tax advice.

A simple routine that works

  1. Pick one or two apps you find easy to use.
  2. Do any quick daily activities in a few minutes.
  3. Check for surveys that fit your profile once or twice a day.
  4. Only start bigger offers when you've read the requirements and have time to finish.
  5. Cash out whenever you reach the minimum.

Frequently asked questions

Why didn't a game offer credit me?

The most common reasons are not being a new user of that game, missing the deadline, using a different device than the one you clicked from, or an ad blocker interfering with tracking. Check the offer terms, then contact the rewards app's support with screenshots of your progress.

Is it safe to link my PayPal account?

Reputable rewards apps typically only need the email address associated with your PayPal account to send payouts. You should never need to share your PayPal password.
