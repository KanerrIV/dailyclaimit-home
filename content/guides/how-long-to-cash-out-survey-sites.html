"When do I actually get paid?" is the question most people ask after their first week on a survey or rewards site. The honest answer is that it depends on four things: how far you are from the cash-out minimum, how long your earnings sit as pending, whether your first cash-out needs verification, and which reward you pick. This guide walks through each one so you know what to expect, and how to check the current rules before you start.

The short answer

Getting your first reward happens in stages, and each stage has its own clock:

  1. Reach the minimum. Your available balance has to hit the platform's cash-out threshold.
  2. Wait for pending earnings to credit. Some rewards show up right away; others sit as "pending" until the advertiser or survey partner confirms them.
  3. Pass any checks. Your first redemption may trigger identity or payment account verification.
  4. Processing and delivery. After you redeem, the platform processes the reward and sends it by email or to your PayPal account.

Nobody can promise a fixed timeline for all of this, and you should be wary of anyone who does. How fast you reach the minimum depends on how many surveys and tasks you qualify for, which varies a lot from person to person. The rest is set by each platform's terms, which can change.

1. Cash-out minimums (thresholds)

Almost every platform makes you reach a minimum balance before you can redeem. That number is the biggest factor in how long your first cash-out takes, so look it up before you sign up.

  • Minimums can differ by reward type. A gift card might be available at a lower amount than PayPal, or the other way around.
  • Your first cash-out can be different. For example, InboxDollars' help center says a first payment request needs at least $15 in your account, and later requests can be made at $10 or more (checked October 2026; it can change).
  • Thresholds can change. BigCash's terms of service say minimum cash-out thresholds may be adjusted based on account behavior, so the amount shown in your own app is the one that counts.
  • Points need converting. On points or coin platforms, check how many points the reward you want costs, not just the headline "minimum."

2. Pending and crediting periods

"Pending" means the platform has tracked your activity but hasn't confirmed it yet. Pending earnings usually can't be redeemed, so they don't count toward your minimum until they credit.

  • Quick tasks often credit faster. Short polls and quizzes tend to credit sooner than offers that depend on an advertiser confirming something.
  • Offers, games, and shopping take longer. Advertisers may need to confirm a purchase, a game milestone, or that a trial wasn't cancelled. Swagbucks' help center, for example, says shopping cash back can take 32 to 75 days from the purchase date to be awarded, with some exceptions (checked October 2026).
  • Check the estimate. Many platforms show an expected credit date in your activity or pending section. Look there before assuming something went wrong.
  • Keep proof. For bigger offers, screenshot the offer terms and your completion screen. It makes a missing-credit support request much easier.

3. First cash-out verification

Many platforms run extra checks the first time you redeem, or whenever something looks unusual. This is a normal fraud-prevention step, but it can add time.

  • Identity verification. Swagbucks says it may ask members to verify their identity through a partner called Persona, usually with a photo of a government ID and a selfie. It says this usually takes a few minutes, but review can take up to 10 more business days (checked October 2026).
  • Matching account details. Use your real name and keep your email and address current. A mismatch with your PayPal account is a common cause of delays.
  • One account, no VPN. Multiple accounts, VPNs, and automated tools break most platforms' terms (BigCash's terms prohibit all three) and can lead to a blocked cash-out or a closed account.
  • Answer honestly. Inconsistent survey answers and speeding can get earnings reversed, which pushes your balance back below the minimum.

Only share ID with the platform's own verification process. A reward site should never ask for your bank login or card PIN. More on that in protecting your privacy.

4. PayPal vs. gift cards: options and timing

Once you redeem, the reward still has to be processed and delivered. Times are usually listed in business days, so weekends and holidays don't count.

  • PayPal needs a matching, verified account. Both Swagbucks and InboxDollars say your name and email need to match your PayPal account, and that the PayPal account must be verified (for example, with a linked bank account). Set that up before you cash out, because PayPal's own verification can take a few days.
  • Gift cards are usually digital. Codes arrive by email or in your account. Check your spam folder and the platform's "my rewards" page if you don't see one.
  • Published timing examples (checked October 2026; can change): Swagbucks' help center asks members to allow up to 10 business days for electronic gift cards and PayPal transfers, noting they usually arrive sooner. InboxDollars' help center says gift card and PayPal processing can take up to 10 business days, with possible delays around holidays, weekends, or when an account is under review.
  • Choose carefully. InboxDollars says processed payments can't be reversed or refunded, and it allows only one pending payment at a time. Double-check the reward and the email address before you confirm.

5. Points expiry and inactivity rules

A balance you never cash out isn't worth anything, and some platforms will remove it if you stop using your account.

  • Inactivity can cancel a pending payment. InboxDollars' help center says that if you don't log in or confirm a PaidEmail at least once in the 10 days before your payment is processed, your account is set to inactive and the payment request is removed. It also says accounts with no activity for 6 months are permanently removed and their balance cancelled (checked October 2026).
  • Points can expire. Points and coin programs often have expiry or inactivity rules in their terms, and these change over time. Look for the words "expire," "inactive," or "forfeit" in the current terms.
  • Closing an account usually forfeits the balance. Redeem what you can before you delete an account.
  • Simple habit: redeem soon after you hit the minimum instead of saving up a large balance.

How to check a platform's current cash-out terms

Every number in this guide can change, so take two minutes to check the source before you start. Here's what to look up:

  1. The rewards or cash-out page. Find the minimum for the reward type you want, and whether your first cash-out has a different minimum.
  2. The help center. Search "how long," "pending," "verify," and "PayPal." Official help articles beat third-party reviews, which are often out of date.
  3. The terms of use. Search for "expire," "inactive," "threshold," and "verification."
  4. Your activity or pending page. Check expected credit dates for anything you've already completed.
  5. Your PayPal account. Make sure the name and email match and the account is verified before you redeem.

Signs a "payout delay" is really a scam

  • A fee to release your money. Legitimate survey and rewards sites don't charge you to cash out.
  • Your bank login, card number, or SSN requested to send a small reward.
  • A minimum that keeps moving with no explanation, and no help center or support contact.
  • Overpayment checks with a request to send part of the money back.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my cash-out still pending?

It's usually still processing, waiting on verification, or held because of an account check. Look at the platform's stated processing time (often in business days), check your email for any verification request, and contact support if it goes past that window.

Is PayPal or a gift card faster?

It depends on the platform and the specific reward. Check the delivery time listed for each option on the rewards page. PayPal also requires a verified PayPal account whose details match yours.

Why did my first cash-out take longer than later ones?

First redemptions are often when platforms verify your identity or payment details. Once that's done, later cash-outs may move faster, but that isn't guaranteed.

Can I lose my points if I stop using a site?

On some platforms, yes. Inactivity and expiry rules are in each platform's terms, and they vary. Redeem once you reach the minimum.
